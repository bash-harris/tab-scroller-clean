Test cricket

Test cricket is a format of the sport of cricket with the longest match duration, and is considered the game's highest standard. Test matches are played between national representative teams that have been granted Test status by the International Cricket Council.

History

The first officially recognised Test match began on 15 March 1877 at the Melbourne Cricket Ground between England and Australia. Australia won that match by 45 runs. The term "Test" derives from the idea that the matches are a test of the strength and endurance of the competing sides.

Format

A Test match is scheduled to be played over five days, with each side batting twice in most cases. Each day consists of a minimum of ninety overs, divided into three sessions separated by intervals for lunch and tea. A match that does not produce a result within the allotted time is declared a draw.

The red ball is used in day matches, while the pink ball was introduced for day-night Tests beginning in 2015. The choice of ball affects swing and visibility under floodlights, and has been the subject of considerable debate among players and administrators.
