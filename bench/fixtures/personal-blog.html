Two Weeks Backpacking Through Patagonia

Posted by Marisol Vega · June 28, 2026

I'd been putting off this trip for three years, mostly out of fear I'd underestimated the weather. I hadn't — the wind in Torres del Paine really does knock you sideways, and I lost a hat to it on day two that I never saw again.

The first four days were the hardest, both physically and mentally. My pack felt heavier than it had any right to be, and I second-guessed the whole itinerary somewhere around the second river crossing. But by day five, once the trail dropped down toward the lake, something clicked and I stopped counting kilometers.

Camping near the base of the towers on the seventh night is the memory I keep coming back to. No one in the campsite talked much that evening. You didn't need to.

If you're planning something similar: bring more layers than you think you need, book the refugios early if you want a bed instead of a tent, and don't rush the W trek just to say you did the full circuit. Slower was better every single day.
