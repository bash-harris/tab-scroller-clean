Solid-State Battery Breakthrough Promises Faster Charging

By Elena Torres · Tech Desk

Researchers announced a new solid-state battery design this week that reportedly reaches 80% charge in under ten minutes while retaining over 90% capacity after 1,000 charge cycles, figures that would put it well ahead of current lithium-ion packs used in most electric vehicles.

The team says the key change is a sulfide-based solid electrolyte that suppresses the dendrite formation which has historically made solid-state cells prone to short-circuiting during fast charging.

Independent labs have not yet reproduced the results, and the researchers acknowledged that manufacturing the cells at automotive scale remains an open problem. A pilot production line is planned for next year to test whether the process holds up outside a lab setting.
