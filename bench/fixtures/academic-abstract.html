Effects of Microplastic Accumulation on Coral Reef Ecosystems: A Five-Year Field Study

R. Almeida, T. Nakamura, S. Okoro

Department of Marine Biology, Coastal Research Institute

Abstract

Microplastic contamination in shallow reef environments has increased substantially over the past decade, yet its long-term ecological consequences remain poorly quantified. We conducted a five-year field study across twelve reef sites, measuring microplastic density, coral polyp stress response, and associated fish community composition. Sites with the highest microplastic concentrations showed a 34% reduction in coral recruitment and measurable shifts in polyp feeding behavior. Fish species richness declined most sharply among reef-dependent grazers, suggesting cascading effects beyond direct coral impact. These findings indicate that microplastic load is a meaningful, independent stressor on reef resilience, distinct from and additive to thermal bleaching pressure.

Keywords

microplastics, coral reef ecology, marine pollution, reef resilience, benthic ecosystems
