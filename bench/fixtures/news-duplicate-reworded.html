Senate Backs Multibillion-Dollar Bullet Train Plan in Narrow Vote

By Marcus Feld · The Statewide Ledger · July 14, 2026

A divided state senate narrowly approved a $2.4 billion package Wednesday to build a bullet train linking the capital with the state's busiest port, after years of delay and two prior attempts that never made it to a floor vote.

The 62-38 tally came after a marathon debate session in which several rural senators voiced concern that their districts would see none of the promised economic benefit despite bearing part of the construction footprint.

Rail authority planners expect the earliest usable stretch of track to open in roughly four years, with the entire corridor finished by the early 2030s if the budget holds. Industry groups tied to freight shipping welcomed the news, framing it as relief for clogged interstate corridors.

A senior aide to the governor called it "a generational bet on the state's infrastructure," while a leading critic countered that the fare model still hasn't been made public and that ridership numbers used to justify the bill "assume best-case everything."

Groundbreaking is penciled in for spring, assuming the pending environmental review clears without major objection.
