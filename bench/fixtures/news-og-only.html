State Approves $2.4B Funding for High-Speed Rail Line

By Dana Okafor · Capital Herald · July 14, 2026

State lawmakers voted 62-38 on Wednesday to approve $2.4 billion in funding for a new high-speed rail line connecting the state capital to its largest port city, a project supporters say will cut travel time between the two cities from four hours to under ninety minutes.

The bill, which passed after eighteen months of committee hearings and three failed attempts in previous sessions, allocates funding over a seven-year construction window. Transportation officials say the first segment of track could be operational within four years, with the full route completed by 2033.

Opponents of the measure argued that the cost estimate is likely to grow, pointing to overruns on similar projects in neighboring states. Freight and logistics groups largely supported the bill, citing reduced congestion on parallel highway corridors.

"This is the single biggest infrastructure investment this state has made in a generation," the governor's office said in a statement following the vote. Critics, however, warned that fare pricing has not yet been finalized and that ridership projections may be optimistic.

Construction is expected to begin next spring, pending final environmental review. The rail authority said it will hold public comment sessions in each county the line passes through before finalizing the route.
